# Emberkeep: The Ashen Crown

Open `index.html` directly in a desktop browser. It is a self-contained, offline HTML5 action RPG with embedded artwork and no installation, external requests, or dependencies.

## Play

WASD or arrow keys move. Mouse aims; hold left click or Space to attack. Shift dodges in the movement direction (35 resolve; regenerates). Q drinks a potion (65 vitality). E opens nearby chests, equips dropped weapons or armor, and uses blue stairs. P or Escape pauses. New run confirms before replacing a live adventure. Leaving the tab pauses automatically.

Descend through ten numbered floors across five randomized realms, with two floors per realm. Each realm has its own floor and wall palette, architectural motifs, torch color, ambience, and creature roster: flooded limestone halls, a thornwood castle, a moonlit crypt, a ruined keep, and the Cinder Forge. Stairs change the atmosphere and enemies when entering a new realm and restore vitality on every descent. Cave spiders aim and spit venom that poisons; crypt wizards mark a location for lightning; cinder wizards launch fire that burns. Spell markers show where to dodge before impact. If you remain still for nine seconds, floor cracks warn that the dead are stirring; after twelve seconds two zombies climb out and attack. Moving resets the timer, but attacking does not. Gold and potions collect on approach. Equipment pickups compare their stats with your current gear before equipping; replaced equipment remains on the floor. The final dragon appears only on floor 10 and alternates a telegraphed flame cone and radial fireballs. Kill it to recover the Ashen Crown. Experience increases rank and vitality. Runs are not saved between browser sessions.

## Equipment

Longswords, war axes, flanged maces, and spears have distinct silhouettes both in your hand and on the ground. Longswords recover fastest (0.36 seconds); axes deal more damage with slower recovery (0.46 seconds); maces trade reach for impact (0.42 seconds); spears offer longer reach with slightly lower damage (0.40 seconds). All retain the same aimed melee arc. Rarity and chapter improve power; the equipment panel displays total strike power, reach, and recovery.

Leather, chainmail, and plate armor sit on the knight's torso and shoulders, below the head, and visibly change shape and trim. Every chest contains armor; monsters, barrels, and crates can also drop it. Press E to equip, not automatic pickup. Armor reduces all incoming damage, including spells and dragon flame, by its displayed percentage, capped at 40%. It does not stack or affect dodge invulnerability. Armor persists between floors; new runs begin without armor. The equipment panel and pickup comparison display damage reduction. Treasure chests have domed gold-trimmed lids and locks; they open intact and grant loot. Crates are square, plank-built and crossed with pale braces; they splinter when destroyed and remain distinct from the separate breakable barrels.

## Merchants and inactivity threat

Floors 2, 4, 6, and 8 have a clearly marked merchant in the entrance chamber. Press E nearby to open a safe shop. Each visit stocks one weapon, one armor piece, and five healing potions; item prices are shown in gold, unaffordable purchases explain the shortfall, and stock is finite for that floor. Purchased weapons and armor appear beside the merchant for deliberate E-based equipping, so a purchase never silently replaces current gear. The merchant chamber is a sanctuary: enemies cannot enter, and the player cannot take damage there. Floor 10 is the final dragon floor and has no merchant.

## Sound

Web Audio synthesizes evolving chapter-specific music using four rotating melodic motifs, changing four-bar harmonies, bass layers, syncopated phrase pickups, and softer ambient wind/torch textures. Weapon-specific strikes (steel swish, heavy axe, mace thud, spear rush), distinct creature voices and hit timbres, damage, coins, opening chests, barrel/crate breaks, armor clinks, potion pickup, healing, dodging, venom, lightning, fire and dragon cues all have separate feedback. Effects vary pitch/filter timbre and noise offsets per event independently of gameplay randomness. No external sound files or network requests are used.

**Sound effects** and **Music** are independent on/off buttons with separate gain buses. Music includes the low drone and torch/wind ambience; Sound effects controls every gameplay cue. Both default to on; the shared Volume slider defaults to 55% and affects both. Audio unlocks/resumes on clicks or keys, subject to browser autoplay policy. The status message explains blocked or unsupported audio and provides a retry path. Music/ambience fades on pause or tab departure; event tails finish naturally. Settings last for the current page session.

`DungeonAudio.diagnostic()` reports context state, post-volume analyser RMS, separate music/effects bus RMS, event counts, both mute states, volume, and startup errors. `audio_checks.py` validates gesture startup, real nonzero audio samples, event output, mute silence, unmute, zero-volume silence, and browser suspension recovery in isolated Microsoft Edge. `equipment_checks.py` verifies actual equipped/ground silhouettes, E-driven armor loot/equip/swap, damage reduction, all four independent audio toggle combinations, weapon-specific profiles, and randomized pitch. It saves gameplay and silhouette screenshots and JSON evidence to `D:\AI_Output`. Pass a deployed URL ending in `?test` as its first argument to repeat the checks on the live game. Measurements validate generated browser audio, not human listening or physical speaker output.

## Supplied artwork

All 15 original `sample_images/NoLoRA_*.jpg` files were inspected as full room references, not sprite sheets. A source-art gallery embeds reduced copies of every image. Gameplay floor masonry is cropped from NoLoRA_0008 (390, 240, 510, 320); wall masonry from NoLoRA_0015 (230, 110, 350, 160). The original source files are unchanged. The procedural geometry, actors, objects, telegraphs, and effects are drawn in Canvas to match the supplied torchlit stone aesthetic.

## Generation and diagnostics

Twelve jittered rooms are connected by three-tile-wide corridors forming a spanning chain with extra links. Collision uses the same floor grid as rendering and pathfinding. Every floor has a starting sanctuary, lootable stores, and a reachable exit. Enemies follow a breadth-first distance field. Floor generation selects one of five realm identities with `Math.ceil(floor / 2)`; the tenth floor reserves its final room for a dragon.

Add `?test` to expose read-only `emberkeep.generate(seed, chapter)`, the five realm themes and rosters, floor mapping, merchant schedule, idle timer, and `emberkeep.snapshot()` diagnostics. `tests.cjs` runs seeded connectivity, theme/roster uniqueness, collision, combat, loot, ten-floor progression, idle-threat timing/reset, merchant commerce, sanctuary, and boss checks against the actual embedded game script using Node's built-in VM and assertions. `chapter_theme_checks.py` checks every realm, advances through all ten floors using the real E/stairs input path, captures actual Edge screenshots, and creates a contact sheet in `D:\AI_Output`. No dependency installation is required.

Run `node 3d_dungeons\tests.cjs` from the repository root. With Python Playwright and Microsoft Edge already installed, `python 3d_dungeons\browser_checks.py` exercises actual keyboard controls in an isolated headless browser, saves screenshots and a JSON report to `D:\AI_Output`, and checks offline image loading and browser errors. `python 3d_dungeons\combat_visual_checks.py` verifies telegraphed venom, fire, and lightning attacks, status effects, spell audio, and saves spell screenshots. `equipment_checks.py` captures armor facing and chest/crate screenshots; `audio_checks.py` measures a varied music phrase and synthesized effects. Stairs and boss scenarios use fixture positioning to isolate those mechanics; this is not a claim of a full unassisted playthrough.
