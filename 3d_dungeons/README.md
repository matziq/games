# Emberkeep: The Ashen Crown

Open `index.html` directly in a desktop browser. The HTML5 action RPG and artwork are offline-capable; the five compact sound recordings are bundled beside it in `audio`. No installation, external requests, or runtime dependencies are required.

## Mobile and Android

Phone and tablet layouts use the available viewport height and safe-area insets, with no horizontal page scrolling. The Android app keeps the game inside system-bar and display-cutout insets and follows the device's text-size setting. Short landscape screens give the dungeon the full width and place Gear / Map in a drawer; larger tablets retain the sidebar. Drag the MOVE pad to walk and face the knight; hold Attack with another finger to keep striking. Tap the hall to aim and attack, or tap a nearby chest to open it. Use / Equip opens chests, equips gear, trades, or descends; Dodge and Heal use the same resolve and potion rules as the keyboard. Touch cancellation and tab departure release held input. Gear / Map opens a compact equipment/map drawer and pauses the game; close it and tap Resume journey to continue. Music, sound effects, volume, pause, and new-run controls remain available. Desktop keyboard/mouse controls are unchanged.

The local Android package is `D:\OneDrive\Scripts\games\emberkeep\Emberkeep-Android.apk`; the offline web export is `D:\OneDrive\Scripts\games\emberkeep\Emberkeep.html` with its sibling `audio` folder. Start the adventure with Enter the halls to unlock audio. This is a locally debug-key-signed, installable sideload build, not a Play Store release. Keep `android-debug.keystore` in the private output folder to sign compatible future updates; do not commit it or the APK. Closing unexpectedly can lose up to the latest autosave interval. Saves belong to the current browser profile or Android app only; they do not sync across devices, between the website and APK, or between the website and standalone file. Clearing site/app data or uninstalling can remove the save. Storage errors and invalid saves are shown explicitly; an invalid save is not overwritten unless a new run is confirmed.

## Play

WASD or arrow keys move. Mouse aims; click the hall or hold left click or Space to attack. Shift dodges in the movement direction (35 resolve; regenerates). Q drinks a healing potion; Y drinks a mana potion. E opens nearby chests, toggles torches, equips dropped weapons or armor, collects pedestal treasure, and uses blue stairs. **T** cycles spells with a wand or shared ammunition with a ranged weapon; the cycle and heal keys can be customized. R casts the selected spell. P or Escape pauses. New run confirms before replacing a live adventure.

On touch screens, drag the Move pad to walk and face, tap the arena to attack, hold Attack to keep striking, and tap Use / Equip, Dodge, or Heal. Tap nearby chests to open them. The title screen starts with these essentials and keeps advanced rules grouped under expandable guides. Pause and settings remain available in the compact game header. Keyboard and screen-reader users can focus the game and use the accessible action controls; a separate status summary exposes floor, health, equipment, objective, nearby interaction, and threats, with important changes announced. The minimized map and equipment panel remains alongside play on tablets; phone layouts keep the arena primary and open the panel on demand.

Magic ammunition, scroll, and wand pickup notices use their effect colors; ordinary loot retains its usual color.

Click or tap the arena to aim and attack. A nearby chest opens directly when clicked or tapped; an out-of-reach chest must be approached first. Clicking or tapping nearby gear, a torch, merchant, or stairway also selects it while attacking; **E / Use** acts on that selection. A gold dashed outline identifies the target, and repeated clicks cycle overlapping objects. Use toggles a torch; ordinary attacks do not. Ranged Light, Fire and Fireball hits light torches; Cold spells extinguish them. Attacks start at the knight's center, so overlapping enemies can be hit.

**X / Delete gear** removes only an explicitly selected loose weapon or armor, not equipped gear, treasure, or fixtures. The side selection panel's **Undo last delete** button restores that exact item once. Only the most recent deletion can be undone; another deletion replaces it. Undo does not change gold, equipment, monsters, or any other state. The latest deletion survives stairs, trapdoors, and fissures during the same run: on its original floor the item returns to its original location; after descent it returns safely at the knight's feet. A new run clears undo, and one-use restoration prevents duplication. All selection, Delete, Clear, and Undo buttons support touch.

Descend through **16 numbered floors** across five randomized realms, with two floors per realm and the final realm continuing through the cycle's later floors. Each realm has its own floor and wall palette, architectural motifs, torch color, ambience, and creature roster: flooded limestone halls, a thornwood castle, a moonlit crypt, a ruined keep, and the Cinder Forge. Stairs change the atmosphere and enemies when entering a new realm and restore vitality on every descent. **A boss guards every eighth floor**; the boss must fall before any route down opens. Cave spiders fire instant tracking webs that root you, then rush forward at high speed for an immediate melee strike; crypt wizards mark a location for lightning; cinder wizards launch fire that burns. Spell markers show where to dodge before impact. If you remain still for nine seconds, nearby floor markers warn that the dead are stirring; after twelve seconds zombies climb out beside you, extinguish every torch in your room, and attack. Moving resets the timer, but attacking does not. Gold and consumable drops collect on approach. Successful strikes show green damage values over enemies; actual health lost shows red values over the knight, accounting for armor and invulnerability. Nearby notifications stack, fade, and stay within the playfield. Equipment pickups compare their stats with your current gear before equipping; replaced equipment remains on the floor. Experience increases rank and vitality. The final boss on Floor 16 opens the next harder cycle; equipment, inventory, rank, gold, and crowns carry forward.

The knight stays upright in every direction: left-facing art is a horizontal mirror, not a rotated body. Weapons and attack arcs still aim independently in any direction.

## Shifting halls and room identities

Every floor includes eight distinct ordinary room identities, layered over its existing realm palette: a graveyard, provision stores, a winery, weapon armory, armor vault, silken nest, overgrown abandoned chambers, and ancient sanctuary. Their furnishings identify them visually. **Only Entrance Hall is labeled**, in the world and below the realm heading; other rooms and connecting passages have no room-name labels. Furnishings do not block movement or change the connected floor grid, but stop projectiles until broken. Only melee and projectile attacks destroy them, for exactly **1-2 coins**, once; no equipment or potion rewards come from decor. They never enter the item chooser or respond to E / Use. Breaking webbed eggs removes their associated slowing web patch. Torches, stairs, and merchants are not destructible.

Repeated bone glyphs, carpet stripes, colored floor-tile outlines, and other decorative tile marks have been removed for a cleaner stone floor. The supplied stone texture and masonry wall edges remain intact. Expired traps no longer show SPENT ovals. Active and SAFE traps, glints, inspection/countdown feedback, exit cues, and the actual earthquake cracks remain visible. Fissures retain their formation warning and gold-edged crack art without a repeated permanent floor label.

Earthquakes begin on **floor 3**. Floors 1-2 never quake. On non-boss floors, an occasional earthquake shakes the view and sounds a low rumble, then opens a gold-edged fissure after a three-second warning. The first can happen after 38-60 active seconds outside a merchant sanctuary; subsequent events are 45-70 active seconds apart, with at most three fissures per floor. Fissures have a brief opening grace period, remain visible on the floor, and drop you **exactly one floor** when walked or dodged across. They reuse the trapdoor descent path: input releases, vitality restores, and realm and merchant rules apply at the new entrance. Floors 8 and 16 never spawn pits or fissures; every other multi-floor descent is capped at the next boss floor, so no drop can skip a boss. Merchant sanctuaries suppress tremors; pausing and shopping do not advance these timers.

## Equipment

Crossbows fire fast bolts (720 units/second, 0.85-second recovery, longer reach and heavier base strike) and appear in normal equipment loot and merchant stock. All ranged weapons have unlimited regular shots. Chests always drop one random five-round special-ammunition pack; ordinary eligible loot sources have an 18% chance. All eight ranged weapons share one ammunition inventory and selection. Picking up ammo adds stock without changing the selected type, including regular. **T**, or the cycle button on touch, selects regular ammunition or an available special type. Each successful ranged attack consumes one special round; cooldown and pause consume none. Switching to a wand or melee weapon drops every special round as floor packs preserving their exact counts; only ranged weapons can collect ammo.

Fire and Poison rounds add four seconds of burning/poison to the first creature hit. Explosion rounds burst for 35 additional damage within 75 units, respecting walls and damaging breakable obstacles. Lightning chains damage through nearby visible enemies; Web roots the first target; Charm temporarily turns a hit creature from the player. Light ammunition damages up to three distinct monsters, carries a soft, wall-bounded halo in flight, and leaves one wider pool of light where it stops for five active gameplay seconds. These effects work on every ranged weapon, including thrown gear and firearms; pause does not age lingering effects. Walls and intact objects stop light rounds. Light and Fire ammunition light a torch on impact.

Longswords, war axes, flanged maces, spears, and halberds have distinct silhouettes both in your hand and on the ground. Longswords recover in 0.36 seconds; axes deal more damage with slower recovery (0.46 seconds); maces trade reach for impact (0.42 seconds); spears offer longer reach (0.40 seconds); halberds have the longest melee reach and heavier strikes (0.57 seconds). Longbows fire fast arrows (0.65 seconds), throwing spears launch heavier javelins (0.72 seconds), throwing knives recover rapidly with lighter damage (0.30 seconds), throwing axes spin with heavier impact (0.66 seconds), boomerangs return toward the knight (0.80 seconds; one in flight at a time), and black-powder rifles and pistols are ranged weapons offering long and quick firearm shots. Ranged weapons consume selected special ammo first, then use unlimited regular shots.

Player and enemy projectiles normally stop at the first living creature or container, including closed chests and container remains; light ammunition is the monster-piercing exception. A shot that breaks a crate/barrel still stops there. A **second hit** clears broken barrels and boxes completely; opened chests can likewise be destroyed by a hit. Clearing remains gives no additional loot. Closed chests remain protected and must first be opened by tapping/clicking nearby or with E / Use. Melee, projectiles, and offensive scrolls can clear remains; later shots pass the cleared spot. Ordinary boomerangs turn back on their first outbound impact; light boomerangs can pierce monsters without hitting the same one twice. Stone stops projectiles. All fourteen weapon types appear in ordinary loot and merchant stock, with distinct held and ground silhouettes. Aim with the mouse on desktop or face/tap the target on touch; Attack uses the equipped weapon. Rarity and chapter improve power; the equipment panel displays total strike power, reach, and recovery.

All loose drops, including gold, potions, gear, ammo, and scrolls, disappear after **30 active seconds**. Incompatible armor, ammo, and scrolls dropped when changing weapons disappear after **10 active seconds**. Both blink during their final five seconds. Equipped and carried items do not expire; pause and shopping freeze ground-item timers. Saved runs preserve remaining time, and Delete/Undo preserves the item's remaining lifetime instead of granting a fresh timer.

## Living nests and unstable chambers

After hatching, spiderlings rush to the room center whether or not the knight stays. Once every surviving spiderling arrives, they combine into a new giant; the existing adult remains separate. The giant's vitality and damage are multiplied by **1 + survivors / 9** (18 survivors yield 3x). Dead spiderlings do not contribute, and destroying all eggs or babies prevents the giant. No kill rewards are awarded for absorbed babies, and their positions and remaining countdown persist in saves. Giant web strikes deal the giant's damage and root the knight.

Crossing the midpoint of a connecting passage activates the nearer room; entering a silken nest this way starts a **four-second** egg-hatching countdown, shown by amber rings and a warning. Each surviving egg sac hatches **three** small spiderlings with 10 vitality, moving toward the room center at 245 units/second (faster on Hard). Destroy sacs or spiderlings before they merge; intact eggs hatch only once, even after leaving and returning. Hatching removes the corresponding web patch. Spiderlings never award loot. Adult spiders strike after a **0.08-second** windup anywhere in their shown attack cone. The web has no projectile travel and roots for one second, including dodge displacement; a hit sends the spider rushing in for a melee attack. Walls, containers, leaving the cone, dodging/invulnerability, and merchant sanctuary can prevent the root. Timers freeze while paused.

On non-boss floors, each ordinary room has a seeded **4%** chance to be unstable. Entrance/merchant and stair rooms are excluded. Time advances only while the knight is inside the unstable room; after seven seconds, an amber square warns for two seconds before a tile opens into a dark, gold-edged pit. Later collapses are six seconds apart, with at most three holes per unstable room. Tiles stay away from stairs and intact furnishings. Entering or dodging across a pit descends **exactly one floor**, releases held controls, and uses the existing safe floor-arrival path. Floors 8 and 16 have no collapsing rooms/pits, and other multi-floor drops cannot skip the next boss gate.

Crates and barrels can contain fruit, pie, or meat. Collecting food adds 15 percentage points of damage reduction for 30 active seconds; another food refreshes the timer. The bonus applies beyond the 40% equipment cap, is shown in the equipment panel, and persists through saves and floor changes.

Hard difficulty adds two ordinary monsters per populated room and increases newly spawned monster speed by 25%. Ordinary non-chest loot chances are halved; chests and boss rewards retain their guarantees. Easy and Medium keep their previous enemy counts and speeds. Changes to room population take effect on the next floor.

The Node suite checks all eight ranged weapons with all seven special-ammo types, class-specific pickup and dropping, automatic regular fallback, and pause/cooldown guards.

## Enemy factions and crowned cycles

Ordinary enemies cannot damage or poison/burn other enemies. Enemy shots are still blocked by creatures and intact containers, but an allied creature takes no damage/status from the impact. Enemy projectiles expire at the radius of their displayed attack cone. Enemy melee, charge, lightning, web and boss attacks target the knight. Hidden traps and their swarms target the knight only; disarmed traps automatically triggered by monsters target monsters, while manually activated hazards can affect both sides.

**Bosses guard Floors 8 and 16** in each 16-floor cycle; their forms rotate among dragons, necromancers, wizards, and empowered versions of other monsters on harder cycles. Every boss has melee and ranged attacks and summons up to four weaker, related minions. A living boss seals every route down; traps, collapse pits, and fissures cannot bypass it, and any multi-floor fall from below is capped at the next boss floor. Kill it to open the stairs and collect a reward cache: about **1,000 gold**, a powerful weapon and armor, three spells, and special ammunition. A brief tune and shower of gold-piece effects celebrate the victory without interrupting play; after Floor 16, the final stair starts a harder cycle with your gear and progress intact. Each additional cycle adds 25% of base enemy vitality/damage and +5 weapon power; speed/timing stay unchanged. Health refills on floor arrival as usual. A new run explicitly resets cycles/crowns.

Subtly mismatched floor tiles mark **crossfire traps**. Eight arrows—ordinary, fire, poison, or lightning—rush from both sides of the room toward the tile, dealing a one-time 50%-of-max-health hit to each eligible target. Disarm the trap first to make it target monsters instead. Stand still beside a **glowing pedestal** for two active seconds to attempt disarming it: the one attempt has a 10% chance of success. Failure immediately releases its boulder; claiming it without inspecting also releases one. A boulder enters from the room's right side, rolls left continuously, and tracks the knight only vertically. It crumbles at a wall or the room edge; five melee hits or ten ranged hits destroy it regardless of weapon strength. Dodge it: contact removes 90% of current health. Another pedestal cannot be claimed while a boulder is active. Boulder and trap state persist in saves.

Run `python 3d_dungeons\crown_checks.py` for desktop/phone friendly-fire, activated-trap, actual boss-kill, uninterrupted celebration, and next-cycle controls. It accepts a deployed `?test` URL. Fixtures isolate mechanics rather than claiming an unassisted full playthrough.

## Mana, scrolls and spellbooks

**Gear / Map** has an on/off auto-equip button and a button cycling **Ranged / Magic / Melee** preference. Eligible upgrades within 3.75 tiles and clear sight fly toward the knight before equipping, leaving the old item on the floor. Ranged includes wands and other ranged weapons; upgrades must improve strike power, with ties favoring faster recovery, then longer reach. Magic preference equips a first wand even when its strike power is lower, then compares wands as usual. Compatible armor upgrades by reduction. Gear preferences are saved with the run.

Dropped **gold and potions** move toward the knight within 3.75 tiles. Ammo does so only with a ranged weapon, and scrolls and spellbooks only with a wand; eligible gear flies toward the knight when auto-equipment is enabled.

Run `python 3d_dungeons\loot_tuning_checks.py` for desktop and phone browser checks of pickup boundaries, collection, item lifetimes, and pause/shop timer protection. It accepts a deployed `?test` URL.

Open the **Spells** panel, choose a spell and scroll or spellbook, then **right-click the game canvas** to aim and cast while holding a wand. **T** cycles stocked spells or special ammo; **R** casts. Scrolls are single-use; rarer spellbooks are stronger and have five uses, displayed in the panel. Neither uses mana. Changing to a non-wand drops carried scrolls and books, preserving book uses.

Wands come in Fire, Fireball, Lightning, Poison, Web, Light and Cold varieties. Each channels a wall-bounded cone every 0.22 seconds while held, costing 10 of 100 mana per pulse. Mana regenerates at 3 per active second; Y or the touch Mana button consumes a potion to restore up to 65. At less than 10 mana the wand waits until enough returns. Mana and potions persist in saves; pause does not advance regeneration. Wands require robes.

Run `python 3d_dungeons\scroll_checks.py` for actual Edge desktop/phone/landscape right-click, R, selection and touch casting, all four effects, pickup, consumption, pause guards, context-menu scope and screenshots. It accepts a deployed `?test` URL. The Node suite covers scroll loot, new-run reset/floor persistence, wall/obstacle blocking, late-arriving poison targets, and Light expiry.

Fireball bursts into layered flame and sparks; Lightning flashes as a branching, glowing bolt. These are visual effects only: damage, range, targeting, and consumption are unchanged. ## Traps and torchlight

Each generated hidden glint has an 8% seeded chance to contain a **Hidden Hoard** weapon when safely inspected. Its floor-level weapon roll gains exactly +5 strike power; the gold reveal and normal selectable pickup invite inspection. A treasure reveal permanently consumes the trap without arming, damage, spawning creatures, or descent. Walking onto an uninspected glint still triggers the original trap and forfeits its hidden reward. Rewards cannot repeat; eighth-floor boss gates remain sealed.

Tiny gold floor glints shine for 0.9 seconds in each 3.6-second cycle, then disappear completely. Hidden traps only glint where combined radial torch illumination exceeds 20%; the knight's navigation halo never exposes them. The HUD does not disclose an invisible glint, although ongoing inspection keeps its progress feedback. Stop **beside** a torchlit trap (within 72 world units, but outside its 23-unit trigger radius) for two uninterrupted seconds to reveal and disarm it. A progress ring confirms inspection; moving or stepping away resets it. Hidden traps ignore monsters and trigger when the knight crosses them.

Disarmed traps reset automatically for monsters: the knight remains safe when a monster triggers one. Press E or tap Use to activate a disarmed trap manually. Creature traps summon allies that fight monsters for 10 active seconds and vanish in a visible poof; their remaining time survives saves and pauses. Manually triggered hazards can affect both the knight and monsters in their area: fire burns, poison damages over time, quicksand slows, and explosions and lightning strike both. Manual trapdoors drop the knight one floor. Bed-bug traps release harmless bugs that visibly cling to eligible targets for 10 active gameplay seconds, without damage or penalties; their remaining time also survives saves and pauses. Trap-origin creatures never drop loot; ordinary monsters retain their rewards. Each trap triggers only once after disarming.

New traps include a large explosion, a seven-second poison cloud, a lightning strike, and short-lived spider or snake swarms. Flood traps seal every passage into a suitable chamber and raise damaging water; attack or shoot any door (180 HP) to break it, open the other doors and drain the chamber. A disarmed flood triggered by monsters targets only them; manually activating it also affects the knight, who can still escape by breaking a door. Flood water, door health, and active trap effects persist in local saves and clear on floor changes. Ambient creaking, rumbling and water drops follow the existing music/ambience mute, volume and pause controls.

Trapdoors send you exactly one floor down when triggered by accident or manually, using the same entrance, healing, realm, merchant schedule, and boss rules as normal descent. Boss floors convert trapdoors into explosion traps and contain no pits or fissures. Entrance sanctuaries and final exit chambers have no traps.

About 30% of ordinary rooms begin fully dark and 40% partially lit; each floor guarantees at least one of each. Entrance and final chambers start lit. Each room has four reachable sconces: dark rooms start with none lit, partial rooms with one, and lit rooms with all four. Torches flicker slowly; some lit sconces burn out a few seconds after the player approaches, and striking them relights them without another automatic burnout. **Strike a torch with any weapon to light or extinguish it; E / Use and selection never toggle it.** Torches cast circular pools with smooth radial falloff and blended overlaps. Radius scales with room area and torch count. Cached visibility polygons stop light at solid walls while permitting natural spill through doorways. Corridors retain their smoothly edged 42% ambient visibility. A small personal halo, visible unlit sconces, minimap, and HUD keep navigation usable without revealing hidden traps. Glints use the same falloff and wall occlusion as rendered light. Merchants, chests, equipment, stairs, and revealed traps retain their E interaction priority. Changes persist for the current floor and in saved runs; floors regenerate on descent.

Body armor, boots, gloves, and helmets have four independent equipment slots and visible worn/ground shapes. Leather, chainmail, and plate body armor sit on the torso and shoulders, below the head; helmets cover the head, gloves the hands, and boots the feet. Every chest contains armor of a random slot; monsters, barrels, and crates can also drop it. Press E to equip deliberately. Replacing a piece only swaps that slot and drops the previous piece. Percentages add across slots, with equipment reduction capped at 40%, including spells and dragon flame; food can temporarily add 15 points beyond that cap. Armor does not affect dodge invulnerability. All slots persist between floors; new runs begin without armor. The equipment panel lists every slot and the combined reduction; pickup comparisons show the matching slot. Treasure chests have domed gold-trimmed lids and locks; they open intact and grant loot. Crates are square, plank-built and crossed with pale braces; they splinter when destroyed and remain distinct from the separate breakable barrels.

Every armor slot follows the equipped weapon: ranged weapons (including guns) allow leather or chain; melee weapons allow chain or plate; wands allow robes. Manual attempts to equip incompatible armor leave it on the ground, and auto-equip ignores it. Changing weapons drops incompatible pieces while retaining compatible ones; older saved runs reconcile incompatible gear onto the ground on resume. Like other loose gear, these pieces eventually expire.

## Merchants and inactivity threat

Merchants also buy carried healing potions for **8 gold each** and any of the four scroll types for **12 gold each**. Separate Sell cards show the carried count and sell exactly one per click/tap; empty inventory disables the button. Sales neither equip gear nor alter merchant purchase stock. Trade is available only in the open merchant shop, and potion resale remains below its purchase price.

Even floors before the cycle finale have a clearly marked merchant in the entrance chamber. Press E nearby to open a safe shop. Each visit stocks weapons for melee, ranged and magic, a robe and other armor, healing potions, special ammunition and scrolls; item prices are shown in gold, unaffordable purchases explain the shortfall, and stock is finite for that floor. Ammunition requires a ranged weapon and scrolls require a wand. Purchased weapons and armor appear beside the merchant for deliberate E-based equipping, so a purchase never silently replaces current gear. The merchant chamber is a sanctuary: enemies cannot enter, and the player cannot take damage there.

The last special-ammunition shot retains its effect, then selection returns to unlimited regular ammunition; T / Cycle still chooses other stocked types explicitly. Explosion ammunition produces a smaller fireball-like burst. Charm ammunition temporarily makes the struck monster attack other monsters; ordinary enemies still cannot harm each other.

Stillness warnings now select reachable, unobstructed points 90-150 world units from the knight, rather than distant random rooms where undead could remain unseen and outside the AI activation range. One or two emerge depending on available space, with 1.35 seconds of visible emergence before they can move or attack; all four sconces in the knight's room go out when they arrive. Other rooms keep their lights, and their sconces can be relit normally. The merchant sanctuary suppresses and resets stillness threats so its safe-room promise remains intact. No warning is shown without a valid local emergence point.

## Sound

The Web Audio score uses an eight-phrase chapter arrangement with bass, sustained harmonies, melodic and bell-like voices, and state-reactive combat, boss, low-health and floor-clear layers. Changes crossfade their musical weights while the arrangement continues. Room-size reverb and high-frequency damping adapt to each realm; positional effects pan and attenuate with in-world distance. Weapon-specific strikes (steel swish, heavy axe, mace thud, spear rush), distinct creature voices and hit timbres, damage, coins, opening chests, barrel/crate breaks, armor clinks, potion pickup, healing, dodging, venom, lightning, fire and dragon cues all retain separate feedback.

Environmental creaks, stone rumbles and water drips also use five short, locally bundled OGG recordings derived from Luka Aleksic's phone-recorded **Various Sound Effects** pack, released as CC0 on OpenGameArt. The selected original recordings are `floor_creak1.wav`, `floor_creak2.wav`, `floor_creak3.wav`, `grind_stone.wav`, and `tap_water.wav`; the files in `audio` are mono Vorbis conversions. The source page and license are documented in `audio/SOURCES.md`. These same-origin assets ship beside the offline HTML and inside the Android APK; no third-party audio host or runtime network access is used.

Realm-specific ambience adds wind, rustling leaves, chains, water echoes and forge embers. It waits during combat, boss fights and low health so warnings remain clear. Bat and snake traps sound wingbeats and a hiss; a flood rushes in when triggered and drains when a door breaks. These are brief positional effects, not looping sounds.

Spell and wand effects, firearms, bow and crossbow releases, arrow impacts, swung weapons, and boomerangs have distinct synthesized cues. Timed trap creatures leave a visible “Poof!” and a puff sound. A rare original synthesized dramatic scream accompanies some humanoid deaths; it is not the copyrighted Wilhelm recording.

**Sound effects** and **Music** are independent on/off buttons with separate gain buses. Music includes the score and recorded environmental ambience; Sound effects controls every gameplay cue. Both default to on; the shared Volume slider defaults to 55% and affects both. Audio unlocks/resumes on clicks or keys, subject to browser autoplay policy. The status message explains blocked or unsupported audio and provides a retry path. Music, ambience and reverb fade on pause or tab departure; event tails finish naturally. Settings last for the current page session.

`DungeonAudio.diagnostic()` reports context state, post-volume analyser RMS, separate music/effects bus RMS, the active music layer, realm reverb, spatial attenuation/panning, bundled-recording events, mute states, volume, and startup errors. `audio_checks.py` validates gesture startup, real nonzero audio samples, recording playback, layered music state changes, distance response, reverb, event output, mute silence, unmute, zero-volume silence, browser suspension recovery, and mobile touch pause/resume in isolated Microsoft Edge. `equipment_checks.py` verifies actual equipped/ground silhouettes, E-driven armor loot/equip/swap, damage reduction, all four independent audio toggle combinations, weapon-specific profiles, and randomized pitch. It saves gameplay and silhouette screenshots and JSON evidence to `D:\OneDrive\Scripts\games\emberkeep`. Pass a deployed URL ending in `?test` as its first argument to repeat the checks on the live game. Measurements validate browser audio routing, not human listening or physical speaker output.
